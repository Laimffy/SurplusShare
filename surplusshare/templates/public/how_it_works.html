How SurplusShare Works

A deterministic, 5-stage reallocation lifecycle combining verified community operations with custom-implemented computer science algorithms.

Stage 1
Donor Posts
Surplus food registered with expiry timestamp
Stage 2
Pantry Requests
Enqueued into Category FIFO queues
Stage 3 (DSA)
Merge Sort
Food sorted earliest-expiry-first (O(n log n))
Stage 4 (DSA)
FIFO Allocation
Fair partial fulfillment (O(1)) without bias
Stage 5 (DSA)
DLL Pickup
Doubly Linked List route traversal & delivery
1

Commercial Surplus Food Registration

Food donors (restaurants, bakeries, caterers, supermarkets) log into the secure Donor Portal. When edible inventory remains unsold at the end of shifts, donors register items with:

  • Standardized food category (Bakery, Vegetables, Fruits, Prepared Meals, Dairy, Dry Goods, Other)
  • Exact available quantity and unit (pieces, kg, liters, servings, packs)
  • Mandatory strict future expiry timestamp (down to the hour and minute)
  • Pickup address and specific storage instructions (e.g., refrigerated, room temp)
2

Community Organization Demand Queuing

Pantries, feeding programs, and shelters register demand according to their operational capacity. Requests enter Category-Partitioned FIFO Queues:

  • FIFO Ordering ($O(1)$ operations): Requests submitted earlier maintain precedence to prevent starvation and ensure equal access.
  • Shelf-Life Filtering: Organizations can specify a minimum acceptable remaining shelf-life (e.g. at least 12 hours) if distribution requires travel time.
  • Fair Partial Fulfillment: If a donor provides 50 units and the top request asks for 30, 30 are allocated and the remaining 20 immediately flow to the next organization in queue.
3

Merge Sort Expiry Prioritization

To prevent food from expiring while waiting in inventory, the system runs a pure Python implementation of Merge Sort ($O(n \log n)$ divide-and-conquer):

  • Chronological Guarantee: Orders listings by expiry timestamp ascending, placing the most time-critical surplus at the very front of the matching pipeline.
  • Stable Sort Property: Listings with identical expiry timestamps preserve their original submission order, ensuring fairness among donors.
  • Automated Expiry Exclusion: Any listings whose expiry timestamp has passed are automatically excluded from the candidate pool.
4

Deterministic Matching & Atomic Commit

The automated matching engine matches sorted food listings with the head of the corresponding category FIFO queue:

  • Evaluates shelf-life constraints and rejects invalid candidate pairs without losing request queue positions.
  • Executes an atomic database transaction: updates listing allocated quantity, marks request status, and records an immutable allocation entry with unique ID (e.g. AL-001).
  • Writes a complete audit log entry documenting the algorithm execution timestamp and run statistics.
5

Volunteer Pickup Routing via Doubly Linked Lists

Pickup stops are linked sequentially as an interactive Doubly Linked List (DLL):

Bidirectional Traversal
Drivers execute routes forward (Head → Tail) and dispatchers review completed routes in reverse (Tail → Head) using prev and next pointer references.
Dynamic Node Swapping ($O(1)$)
If city traffic or urgent store closing hours require stop reordering, dispatchers can swap adjacent stop nodes in $O(1)$ time without rebuilding the array.
Handover & Verification
Upon arrival at the community pantry, the volunteer marks the stop completed. The recipient confirms receipt, updating the live sustainability rescue counter.

Academic DSA Implementation

Every algorithm in SurplusShare is manually implemented in pure Python standard libraries without relying on high-level black-box sorting or external queuing frameworks.
